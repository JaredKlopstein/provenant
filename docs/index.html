Why a local log is not enough

The operator holds the keys

An agent's log is only as honest as whoever holds the disk. Someone with database access and the agent's signing key can rewrite a receipt, relink every hash after it, and re-sign the lot. The result is a chain that is internally flawless.

Below is that exact attack, run against a real store. On the left, Provenant's own local verification. On the right, the same forged chain checked against its external timestamp. Both outputs are copied verbatim from the tool.

What you get

Recording is free. Proving it to a stranger is the paid part.

That split is technical, not artificial. A chain kept on your own disk attests to itself — it cannot rule out that you rewrote it. An external timestamp from an authority you don't control can. That is the only thing we charge for.

Before you rely on this

What Provenant does not do

  • It does not make you compliant with anything. It produces tamper-evident records that support logging obligations. We are not lawyers and this is not legal advice.
  • An anchor does not prove completeness. Receipts written since the last anchor can still be dropped. Anchoring often narrows that window; it never closes it, and the verifier tells you exactly how far the proof reaches.
  • It cannot see what an agent declines to record. Receipts make recorded behaviour tamper-evident. They cannot make unrecorded behaviour appear.
  • A timestamp inside a bundle is not a vote of confidence. Anyone can mint a certificate. Trust comes from a fingerprint you pin yourself, and the verifier reports soundness and trust as two separate answers.
